# News Short Creative Strategy

News Engine treats a short as a visual story, not a narrated article.

## The editorial formula

Each item should make one clear promise and pay it off quickly:

1. **Hook** — a surprising consequence, conflict, reveal, or question in the first second.
2. **Proof** — show the source, image, quote, number, or concrete event that earns trust.
3. **Why it matters** — connect the event to a person, decision, risk, or opportunity.
4. **Turn** — add the next development, contrast, or unresolved question.
5. **Payoff** — answer the promise without repeating the headline.
6. **CTA** — one short invitation to follow, comment, or subscribe.

The writer must give every scene a different information job. If a scene only paraphrases an earlier scene, it should be deleted. The renderer gives each job a different visual treatment: kinetic hook, source/image reveal, staggered evidence cards, and a payoff end card.

## Marketing principles applied

- Lead with the payoff instead of context or an introduction.
- Use a specific audience stake: who is affected and why they should care now.
- Create forward motion by opening a small question, answering it, and opening the next beat.
- Use visuals as evidence or contrast; do not leave the same text card on screen for the whole short.
- Keep captions readable with sound off, but let the image and composition carry part of the story.
- End with one action. Instagram uses a follow/comment frame; YouTube uses a subscribe frame.

These choices follow current platform guidance that emphasizes early hooks, momentum, visual editing, and audience interaction: [Instagram creator best practices](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/), [YouTube Shorts storytelling discussion](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/), and [YouTube Shorts community guidance](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/grow-youtube-channel-interactive-shorts/).

## What to measure after publishing

Review each platform's analytics after enough impressions to compare items:

- viewed versus swiped away — hook strength;
- average percentage viewed — pacing and clarity;
- replays — payoff/loop strength;
- shares and comments — usefulness or emotional relevance;
- follows/subscribes — whether the story built a relationship.

Use those observations to choose the next hook style. Do not optimize only for views; YouTube explicitly recommends identifying what works for your own audience over time.
